# Spray drift and how to reduce it

Source: FAO (2001), Guidelines on good practice for ground application of pesticides; Defra/HSE (2006), Code of Practice for Using Plant Protection Products

## What spray drift is

Spray drift is the movement of spray droplets or vapour away from the target area during or after application. It wastes product, reduces control on the field and can damage neighbouring crops, water and wildlife.

## Main factors

Drift increases with wind speed, smaller droplet size, greater boom height, higher travel speed and higher spray pressure. Hot and dry air makes droplets evaporate and become smaller, which increases drift further.

## Practical measures

Use low-drift nozzles where the label allows, keep the boom as low as practical for the nozzle type, avoid excessive pressure and speed, and stop spraying when conditions change. Leave untreated buffer zones next to sensitive areas as required by the label and local rules.
