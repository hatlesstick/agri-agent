# Temperature inversions

Source: GRDC (Grains Research and Development Corporation), Surface temperature inversions and spraying, fact sheet

## What an inversion is

Normally air gets cooler with height. During a surface temperature inversion a layer of cool air lies near the ground under warmer air. This usually forms on clear, calm evenings and nights and can persist into the early morning.

## Why it matters for spraying

Inside an inversion small droplets can hang in the cool air layer and travel long distances sideways before settling, sometimes damaging crops far from the field. This is why spraying in dead calm conditions is discouraged.

## Signs of an inversion

Typical signs are very low wind or calm, clear skies at night or early morning, mist or fog, dew, and smoke or dust that hangs in a layer instead of rising. If these signs are present, delay spraying until the air mixes, usually after the sun has warmed the ground and a light breeze has started.
