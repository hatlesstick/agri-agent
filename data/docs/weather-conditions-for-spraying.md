# Weather conditions for spraying

Source: FAO (2001), Guidelines on good practice for ground application of pesticides; Defra/HSE (2006), Code of Practice for Using Plant Protection Products

## Wind speed

Spray in a steady, light breeze. Very strong wind carries droplets off target, and dead calm is also risky because it often comes with temperature inversions. A light breeze of roughly 1 to 4 m/s is commonly treated as the working range for boom sprayers; always follow stricter limits on the product label.

## Wind direction

Check wind direction before and during application. Do not spray when the wind blows towards sensitive areas such as water bodies, neighbouring crops, gardens, houses or nature areas.

## Temperature

High temperatures increase evaporation of small droplets and the risk of vapour drift, and some products work poorly in cold weather. A moderate temperature range, often around 10 to 25 °C, is generally preferred; the label may set its own limits.

## Rain before and after spraying

Do not spray on wet foliage if the product needs to dry on the leaf, and avoid application when rain is expected before the product is rainfast. Rain shortly after spraying can wash the product off the crop and into watercourses.
